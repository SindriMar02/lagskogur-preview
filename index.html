THREE LANDSCAPES

A river on the Golden Circle, a valley in Borgarfjörður, and a crater on Snæfellsnes.

Since2014

Róbert Ágústsson and Heiðrún Hafliðadóttir live in Borgarnes with their three sons and have built the houses up one at a time. Heiðrún answers the emails.

The Golden CircleBRÚN

A glass-walled house above the Hvítá in the village of Laugarás, drawn by the architect Sigurður Sigurðsson. Four bedrooms for twelve, a roof terrace, and a hot tub the size of a small pool. Geysir, Gullfoss and Þingvellir are all within the hour.

BorgarfjörðurFIVE HOUSES

Nes stands on the Norðurá within walking distance of the Glanni waterfall and the Grábrók crater. Bakki and Holt share a birch wood at Helluskógur, thirty metres apart. Lundur and Klettur stand side by side west of Borgarnes, each with its own tub.

SnæfellsnesHROSSHOLT

FIVE BEDROOMS ON A SECLUDED FARM, WITH THE ELDBORG CRATER IN THE DINING ROOM WINDOW AND THE GLACIER DOWN THE ROAD.

All yearROUND

Floor heating in every house, so a winter night in the tub is the point rather than a dare. The northern lights come to the deck; in summer the nights never get dark at all.

ThreeRIVERS

THE HVÍTÁ AT BRÚN, THE NORÐURÁ AT NES, THE LANGÁ AT BAKKI. THREE OF THE HOUSES STAND ON A RIVER YOU CAN HEAR FROM THE TUB.

A houseAPART

Between an hour and two from Reykjavík, every one of them off its own track, with nothing in the window but the landscape it was built to look at.

Seven hot tubs

each facing its own view

Brún's takes twelve people. The cottages' take seven. All of them are on the deck of the house, heated all year, with the grill and the outdoor seating beside them and the landscape in front.

THE POINT IS NOT THE TUB. It is that at every one of the seven, the last thing you do in the evening is sit outside in warm water and look at the place you came to see.

Seven houses, from Reykjavík

7 dots to hover

Laugarás, on the Golden Circle. Twelve people, four bedrooms, a roof terrace over the Hvítá and a walk to the Laugarás lagoon. See Brún

Bifröst, on the Norðurá. Ten people in four bedrooms plus a guest house, with the Glanni waterfall and the Grábrók crater on foot. See Nes

Helluskógur in Borgarfjörður. Eight people, three bedrooms, drawn by the architect Sigurður Hallgrímsson, with the Langá below. See Bakki

Helluskógur, thirty metres from Bakki. Six people in a timber cottage with a tub for seven; book the two together for fourteen. See Holt

West of Borgarnes. Ten people across a cabin and its guest house, four bedrooms, a lake and a mountain in the window. See Lundur

Beside Lundur. Six people in three bedrooms; the two houses together take sixteen with a tub each. See Klettur

A farm on Snæfellsnes. Twelve people in five bedrooms, the Eldborg crater from the dining room and the glacier down the road. See Hrossholt
